# Detail, tone and source clocks

Keep wider original context alongside tight detail. Cropping can hide the lap-wide
base that distinguishes a keyboard from a phone, remove the feet needed to identify
an exercise, or obscure where a cleaning tool contacts the floor. For a decisive
small feature inspect individual images; shrinking a large contact sheet can erase
useful native detail. Enlargement shows existing pixels, not newly recovered keys,
text or texture. For a tiny hand/object interaction, use `layout="detail"` with a
tight crop including both hands and their target, and `zoom=4` (up to8 if useful).
This supplies one native context frame per page and compact enlarged temporal crops.
Compare consecutive frames across the movement, not just a larger isolated still.
Revisit wider frames when body rotation, a hidden supporting hand or a carried
object could explain apparent movement against the background. Compare original
tone with gamma/contrast variants; magnification makes compression artifacts larger
as well as real features. Follow all pages of the selected interval.

Choose a display transform for a specific visibility problem. Gamma can reveal dark
midtones; contrast stretch can expose a narrow intensity range; CLAHE can improve
local contrast; sharpening can emphasize existing edges; rotation can make a pose
or document easier to inspect. Read the tool's parameter convention: in this
harness gamma greater than 1 brightens. Historical scripts may use the opposite
exponent convention. Compare transformed views with originals; stretch, CLAHE and
sharpening can amplify compression blocks and create misleading apparent edges.
Rotation does not change the actor's physical orientation. Saturation, occlusion
and absent field of view remain information limits. Do not use imagined detail,
generative reconstruction or plausible-looking artifacts as observations.

## What each source can establish

IR shows reflected infrared brightness, not temperature or visible-light color.
Use it for scene appearance and interaction sequences, without identifying material
from brightness alone. Thermal shows temperature-related contrast, not calibrated
temperature; its palette is not clothing color and does not establish illness,
exertion or emotion. It can preserve useful body movement or longer coverage when
IR is short. Follow visible hand/forearm placement through approach, hold and return,
without treating general arm placement as evidence of finger manipulation.

Plain Depth can clarify posture, limb trajectories and spatial changes. Its
intensity is not verified metric distance, and it supplies no ordinary texture.
Sparse contours or rendering holes are not object boundaries. Small objects can
merge with hands or surfaces in Depth and Thermal; proximity alone does not prove
contact, and an occluded limb does not establish stillness. Judge resolved features,
not a fixed source ranking. Another modality need not settle an object identity.

Follow a handled item's shape, parts, orientation and resulting state where visible.
Keep continuity of observations without carrying an earlier speculative object label
into a later interaction. Distinguish movement in place from travel and a sustained
body contact from free-space handling.

## Clocks and movement quality

Keep each stream on its own clock. Equal durations, rates or timestamps do not prove
alignment. Compare distinctive visible transitions in representative frames before
relating times. If a likely offset appears, verify it at multiple events and retain
uncertainty; do not apply an offset learned from another recording. Merge matching
events, not timestamps alone. A thermal-only late episode remains useful even when
an exact cross-stream time conversion is unavailable.

Contact sheets are sampled images, not continuous playback. Native timestamps help
with order but sparse images do not reliably establish smoothness, acceleration or
rhythm. For a motion-quality claim inspect consecutive native frames and transitions;
do not derive it from contact-sheet spacing or whole-clip duration. Distinguish
frames extracted, delivered and actually inspected when discussing coverage.
