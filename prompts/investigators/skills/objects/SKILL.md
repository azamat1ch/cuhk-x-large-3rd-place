# Interaction first, dataset wording second

Establish which object the question targets through observed handling, destination,
scale and shape. Share that evidence with action questions. A visible interaction
can support the intended name without resolving fabric texture, keys or publication
type. Clearly identify a training naming convention when it decides an ambiguous name.

Training-supported conditional preferences:

- Wiping: cloth rather than napkin/sponge. Do not confuse cloth with clothes.
- Garment handling/folding/dressing: clothes rather than blanket.
- Paper/page handling: documents rather than book, magazine, newspaper or notebook.
- Ear-directed headphone handling: headphones rather than headset/speaker.
- Furniture opening: cabinet rather than shelf/drawer/fridge, if that interaction
  is supported; distinguish the furniture from clothes being handled inside it.
- Broad keyboard/base operation: keyboard; mouse is an observed naming distractor.
- Drinking-container object questions: glass rather than cup/mug/bottle is the
  observed convention. It does not say bottles cannot occur in pouring videos.
- Phone/smartphone and remote are all real labeled classes. Use the supplied wording
  and discriminating interaction; never default every electronic object to phone.

Read [training-distribution](references/training-distribution.md) for the complete
training inventory and counts conditional on every offered object. They are
in-sample label statistics, not visual measurements, calibrated probabilities,
exhaustive ontology, hard elimination rules or guarantees for a new question.
Even an option always correct when offered in training must fit this interaction.
Zero correct does not mean physically absent or impossible in other task categories.
