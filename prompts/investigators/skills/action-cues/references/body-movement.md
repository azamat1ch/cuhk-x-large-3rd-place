# Posture, locomotion and exercise

| Action | Useful distinction to inspect |
| --- | --- |
| walk | Sustained alternating steps that relocate the person or a clear enacted walking episode. Distinguish incidental steps while dressing/cleaning; walking can also overlap those actions. |
| lunges | Split-leg stance with one leg forward/back and a lowering bend, then recovery or alternation. Inspect both knees/feet; a side crop can hide the split stance. |
| sit down | Body descends onto a seat/support from a higher posture. Being seated at the first frame is not evidence the transition occurred in the clip. Track pelvis/seat relationship. |
| lie down | Body moves into a horizontal or reclined supported posture. Distinguish the transition from starting reclined, bending at the waist, or reaching toward the floor. |
| stand up | Body rises from seated, crouched or reclined support into standing. Standing throughout is a state. A small torso lift or reaching overhead is not sufficient. |
| stretch | Sustained or controlled extension of limbs/torso, often held or repeated with planted feet. Compare with a lunge's split-leg descent, squat, massage or jumping-jack cycle. |
| jumping jacks | Coordinated legs spreading/closing with arms rising/lowering over successive cycles. Acted movement can be abbreviated; inspect both upper and lower body rather than arm swings alone. |
| squats | Largely symmetric knee/hip flexion and lowering then rising with feet planted. Distinguish from lunges and from actually settling onto a chair. |
| run | Alternating leg cycles with running-like cadence/body mechanics, including an acted in-place episode. Fast arm motion alone is insufficient; inspect feet and weight transfer. |
| balance on one leg | One foot lifts while weight stays on the other through a deliberate hold, possibly with arms out. Distinguish the sustained support pattern from a normal walking step or brief clothing adjustment. |

Posture transitions are changes, not labels for all activity in that posture. A cut,
occlusion or missing early segment can leave a transition unsupported; explain the
coverage limit. For repeated exercise, identify the geometry before interpreting pace.
Slow movement does not by itself mean calm, methodical, careful or smooth execution.
