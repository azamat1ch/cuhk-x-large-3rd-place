# Desk activities and electronic devices

| Action | Useful distinction to inspect |
| --- | --- |
| type | Fingers/hands repeatedly contact a broad base, often under a raised panel. Keep torso/lap scale: a lap-spanning keyboard base can look like a phone when tightly cropped. Quiet wrists do not exclude finger activity. |
| write | Usually a tool-bearing hand makes small marks/tracing strokes on a supported page/surface, often stabilized by the other hand. Some question wording uses writing broadly enough to include typing: when a keyboard interaction is visible, consider it as a candidate for an offered Writing label rather than requiring a visible pen. Record the physical interaction as typing and distinguish that observation from the possible wording match. If writing and typing are separately offered, discriminate them. |
| call | A phone-like object held at an ear or an otherwise supported calling sequence. Compare one ear-directed hand with both-ear headphone handling. Call means a phone call, not shouting to someone. |
| check time | A wrist is raised/rotated toward the gaze, sometimes touched by the other hand, followed by lowering. Reading the watch face is unnecessary. Look for directed gaze and a deliberate inspect-and-release episode; a longer hold can make these cues clearer, but duration alone is not diagnostic. Compare a brief wrist movement with sleeve adjustment, reaching or a transitional glance. A brief clear inspection still counts. Preserve repeated checks. |
| read | Sustained gaze toward paper/pages with supporting handling; stillness alone is insufficient. A phone screen and a paper surface require context. Do not invent text or document content. |
| turn a page | A sheet edge lifts/crosses and settles, with a visible page-state change. Contrast with wiping over stationary papers or lifting/repositioning the whole notebook. Inspect successive frames. |
| take selfie | A phone-like object is held away from the body and oriented toward the face, sometimes followed by a press/pose. Compare screen use near the torso and remote pointing toward a display. Device verticality alone does not establish selfie or phone identity. |
| play board games | Repeated small piece placement/movement on a tabletop playing area, potentially with alternating attention between pieces/regions. Compare discrete placements with pen strokes, typing or general object sorting. Board detail may remain unresolved. |
| watch TV | Attention toward a display/consistent viewing direction, possibly preceded by remote pointing/pressing and followed by watching. A press alone does not establish a sustained watching episode; the screen itself may be out of view. |
| use phone | Handheld screen-directed looking/tapping/swiping, usually with a compact object. Distinguish from typing on a separate base, a remote press, a call at the ear or selfie pose. Use the question's offered level of specificity. |
| listen with headphones | Ear-directed placement/handling and a supported listening episode. Already wearing headphones is a state, not proof of onset. Track interaction/task changes, but do not require that listening starts last or after all other activities. |

For listening, record visible accessory state separately from candidate activity
episodes. An earcup adjustment or pause alone does not prove playback; absence of
a new adjustment does not disprove ongoing listening. Retain supported early episodes
and uncertain onset rather than forcing an order from the expected routine.

For devices, combine scale, hand position, orientation and target. Phone and remote
are genuine competing classes. A upright rectangle, one-handed grip or an option's
training frequency cannot settle the interaction on its own. Joint action/object
answers should use the same supported episode and acknowledge naming uncertainty.
