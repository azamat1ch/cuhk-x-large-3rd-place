# Cleaning, garments and furniture

| Action | Useful distinction to inspect |
| --- | --- |
| sweep | Floor-directed strokes moving debris toward an area or dustpan, often using a long handle. Tool head, stroke pattern, dustpan and destination jointly help. One-handed grip alone does not prove sweeping. |
| mop | Floor-directed pushing/pulling or swabbing with a handle and broad contact region; tool exchange or a different handling episode can distinguish it from sweeping. Two hands alone do not prove mopping. An occluded tool head may leave genuine uncertainty. |
| wash dishes | A held bowl/plate/cup is rubbed, wiped or cleaned, often turning against a flexible cloth. Acted washing can look like dry wiping/drying with no visible water. Establish the dish-directed interaction; do not require a sink. |
| wipe surfaces | Repeated contact sweeps over a fixed tabletop or other surface, often with a cloth. Compare whether the surface is fixed versus a dish being held/rotated. Stationary nearby papers weaken a page-turn interpretation. |
| fold clothes | A flexible garment is spread, aligned, brought edge-to-edge or compacted through folds. Distinguish a held wiping cloth from a larger garment through handling and scale, not inferred texture. |
| throw away rubbish | An item is carried or swept toward a bin/disposal area and released. Inspect the destination and release; merely lowering a hand behind furniture does not resolve what happened. |
| open a cabinet to get things | A door/panel is moved, followed by reaching into the storage space and possible retrieval. Follow furniture motion and hand path; the object taken may remain hidden. Compare this with handling clothes outside the furniture. |

Tool replacement can establish separate cleaning episodes even when both heads are
hidden. Inspect approach, pickup, exchange and put-down with wider floor context.
Small repositioning steps during cleaning do not automatically constitute a separate
walking action. Do not count one dish-cleaning episode again as surface wiping;
look for a separately supported surface-directed episode.
After dish cleaning, test hand/dish drying and cloth put-down before adding surface
wiping. Low hands, horizontal movement and proximity to a counter do not establish
contact with it. Follow both hands: if the cloth is worked between them, wrapped
around a held object, or moved along with that object, the target is still the
hands/dish. Movement against stationary background landmarks establishes trajectory,
not the contact target: cloth around a carried dish or utensil also crosses those
landmarks, especially as the person turns sideways or lowers their arms.

For a proposed switch from held-item cleaning to surface wiping, inspect the target
change itself. Follow the previously held item and supporting hand through the
transition; look for release/set-down or another visible separation of cloth from
that item, followed by cloth contact with the fixed surface. A low cloth silhouette,
apparent flattening, repeated lateral strokes or body rotation alone do not resolve
that switch. A folded/arched cloth can conceal a held utensil even at counter height.
When the contact interface and supporting hand remain hidden and no target change
is established, keep the episode as continuation of the supported held-item cleaning;
do not add a second surface-wiping label on this evidence. State the unresolved
alternative in uncertainty. This continuity rule applies to an ambiguous transition
out of held-item cleaning, not to an independently visible surface-wiping episode.
