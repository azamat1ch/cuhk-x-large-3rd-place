# Personal care and clothing

| Action | Useful distinction to inspect |
| --- | --- |
| wash face | Hands repeatedly contact/rub the face, often both hands over cheeks/forehead. Separate this from one small mouth-directed implement, hair strokes, or a single face touch. Water need not be visible in an acted sequence. |
| brush teeth | Repeated small strokes at the mouth with a held implement; the contact location and rhythmic hand motion matter more than resolving bristles. Contrast with a cup held for drinking or a brief hand-to-mouth medicine gesture. |
| comb hair | Repeated strokes through or over the hair, often with a consistent held-tool grip. Track path across the head; briefly adjusting an earcup or scratching once is weaker support. |
| undress | Clothing moves off the body: sleeve withdrawal, garment lifted over the head, or removal followed by release. A loose garment held in front of the torso alone cannot establish direction. |
| wipe hands | Hands rub against a held flexible cloth or wipe individually with it. Distinguish a hand-cleaning destination from rubbing the face, folding a garment, or wiping a table. |
| get dressed | Arms/body enter a garment and clothing settles onto the body. Follow before/after sleeve and torso relationships. Can overlap walking; do not force sequential non-overlapping episodes. |
| take medicine | A small-item/pill-container sequence followed by a brief hand-to-mouth gesture, perhaps a separate drink. The pill may be unresolved; describe the actual handling and question support rather than claiming to see swallowing or a tablet. |
| check body temperature | Plausible enactments include an instrument held to the forehead, tucked under an arm, or placed at the mouth, often with a hold and subsequent inspection. These are general possibilities, not verified training gesture frequencies. Compare sustained placement with brushing strokes, a phone at the ear, drinking, or incidental head contact. A device display is not required, and one forehead touch is not conclusive. |
| self-massage | Repeated rubbing, kneading or pressure on one's own body, such as neck/shoulders, arms, torso or legs. Inspect sustained purposeful contact versus a brief adjustment, scratch, temperature check or stretch. Do not require one particular body site. |

For head-directed actions, inspect the hand destination in context: mouth, ear,
hair, forehead and neck can collapse into the same few pixels in a crop. A second
source or consecutive frames can help; repeated brightening cannot uncover an
occluded implement. Do not infer medical condition, temperature, or intention.
