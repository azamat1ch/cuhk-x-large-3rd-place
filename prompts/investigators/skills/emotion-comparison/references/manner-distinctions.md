# Interpret the actual alternatives

These are comparison cues, not fixed speed labels or a psychological diagnosis.
Start with the offered words and the strongest observable contrast. Actors can enact
a manner through modest changes in pace; do not require theatrical mood cues. Ordinary
coordination and continued task engagement fit many options and are weak tie-breakers.

- Quickly/Rapidly/Swiftly/Briskly: compact, fast execution; coordination can be intact.
- Hurriedly/Hastily/Urgently/Impatiently: compressed engagements/transitions, urgency
  or rushing. No requirement for mistakes, dramatic acting or visibly frantic motion.
  When a speed word and a haste word both remain plausible candidates after
  group-option analysis, fast movement alone favors the direct speed description; prefer haste when abbreviated
  task engagement, overlapping transitions or abruptness adds a distinct urgency cue.
- Anxiously/Nervously/Tensely/Restlessly/Frantically: briskness may be relevant, but
  look for tension, repeated adjustment, abruptness or unsettled pauses when separable.
  Forcefully is about force, Eagerly about ready engagement; neither guarantees speed.
- Slowly/Leisurely/Unhurriedly/Lazily: drawn-out comparable movements or discretionary
  engagement; distinguish these from additional required work and prescribed holds.
  More strokes in an open-ended grooming bout can be discretionary engagement,
  not a reason by itself to reject Unhurriedly. Leisurely and
  Unhurriedly do not require lounging, inactivity or conspicuous lingering. Continued
  work does not disqualify them. Slowly can describe repeated measured execution even
  when a few pickups are quick. Compare the broader matched pattern, not one pause.
  When equivalent work takes longer and distinguishing handling cues remain weak,
  prefer this interpretation over ordinary composure, task focus or regular cadence.
- Calmly/Peacefully/Relaxedly/Casually/Naturally: composure or lack of urgency can
  coexist with quick movements. Compare economy, relaxed transitions and pauses.
  Casual execution can remain task-focused. Calmly is not a default for every
  coordinated slow take; distinguish composure from drawn-out execution.
- Patiently versus Comfortably: in staged routines, patience is often enacted as
  measured, unhurried progression through ordinary actions, allowing each step its
  time. Literal waiting for something external or conspicuously prolonged pauses are
  not required. When one sibling rushes and the other deliberately takes its time,
  Patiently is a strong interpretation even if the slower take keeps working.
  Prefer Comfortably when there is additional visible bodily ease or relaxed posture;
  an ordinary pose, coordinated movement or absence of waiting alone does not
  distinguish comfort from patience. Do not let one extra gesture outweigh repeated
  timing differences in common actions. Required holds alone establish neither word.
- Quietly/Soothingly may suggest gentle, restrained handling; silent frames cannot
  establish actual sound or soothing intent.
- Intently/Earnestly: sustained, purposeful task engagement can distinguish these
  from casual execution only when engagement is distinctive. Merely reading, writing,
  looking down, holding an object or following the routine does not establish intent.
  Extra work and longer engagement are not automatically evidence of earnestness.
  In grooming, do not infer Earnestly simply from more ordinary strokes, changing
  hands or covering another hair region. Compare purposeful handling or checking
  separately from time spent. When the main contrast is repeatedly longer bouts at
  similar stroke cadence, Unhurriedly better describes that contrast; any assignment
  of Earnestly to the other member by exclusion should be acknowledged as such.
- Steadily/Evenly: consistency across repetitions and tasks; not automatically the
  middle-speed recording. Regular, continuous progression can support them without
  perfectly measurable repetition counts. A slower sibling can still be Evenly;
  compare repeated regularity against actual drawn-out execution rather than choosing
  Slowly solely because the other sibling rushes. Smoothly/Gracefully concern fluent,
  connected transitions; a slow controlled trajectory alone does not establish them.
- Carefully/Cautiously/Attentively/Meticulously/Precisely: handling, checking and
  placement can support a distinction when unusually deliberate or repeated, beyond
  the action's ordinary requirements. Looking at the object and supporting it with
  both hands are not sufficient. For Meticulously, compare localized fine work and
  repeated coverage against generic inspection of an instrument or container.
- Methodically/Orderly/Neatly/Deliberately: organized transitions, deliberate setup,
  placement and completion. Action order alone does not prove methodical manner.
- Thoroughly/Diligently: sustained task completion or coverage can matter in cleaning
  and personal care. Repeated coverage of additional relevant areas can support
  Thoroughly even without seeing a finished outcome. Do not dismiss such work merely
  because it is not slower motion; a longer workout alone remains nondiagnostic.
- Gently/Softly/Lightly versus Firmly/Forcefully: visible contact/movement force when
  resolvable. Restrained acceleration, gradual contact and supported release may
  suggest gentleness without measured force, but ordinary support or an open hand
  alone is weak. More gradual handling can support Gently/Softly, but these words are
  not synonyms for longer or slower: a slow movement can still be firm. Overall duration
  alone cannot distinguish gentleness from steadiness. Compare repeated approach/contact/release patterns before relying
  on one grip. Low-resolution images may leave these qualities unresolved.
- Absentmindedly/Joyfully/Contently/Confidently/Seriously and similar words carry little
  guaranteed tempo information. Do not manufacture mood from an obscured face.

A single performance can fit several words. Explain why one is better *among these
options*, using sibling contrasts and repeated observations. When two remaining
options are difficult to distinguish, inspect pauses versus action speed,
regularity versus fluidity, or handling versus engagement as appropriate. Do not assign
slow/medium/fast solely from total durations or force every gesture to fit one adjective.
