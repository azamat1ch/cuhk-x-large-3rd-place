# Shared words, distractors and joint assignments

Normalize case and obvious spelling variants for comparison only: Hasitly→Hastily,
Tensly→Tensely, Serioiusly→Seriously. Preserve offered wording and letter mapping.

For uncertain/incompatible candidates, use each full option set independently;
do not use sibling elimination or force distinct assignments. The joint reasoning
below applies only to visually compatible candidates.

Intersect their option words. Exactly three shared words suggest candidate styles;
the nonshared fourth option is often added. Prefer shared candidates and distinct
chosen words when consistent with the visual evidence.
A near-synonym can be the added option; do not exclude a word merely because it is
rare, unusually specific or also a distractor elsewhere.

A triplet with three shared words has six possible ways to give each recording a
different word. Identify the clearest performance first, then inspect the pair whose
words could plausibly be swapped. Two well-supported choices may constrain the third; acknowledge elimination if its visual evidence is weak.
The three words need not represent three different speeds. Often two describe more
measured behavior and one describes rushed execution.

For two compatible recordings sharing three words, consider each possible pairing
of different words: six pairings in total. Any of the three words may be unused,
including the fast/rushed word. Compatibility does not prove that the pair is complete.
Compare a brisk/rushed-versus-measured interpretation with two measured performances and a missing fast sibling. Do not require dramatic haste,
but a relatively faster controlled performance is not automatically hurried. Repeatedly
brief periods of action or rapid transitions can support the brisk/rushed interpretation.
When timing differences are small, compare repeated differences in handling and
rhythm before choosing. Do not assume which word is missing. "Controlled" can be quick, and "calm" does not require slow movement.

When the intersection is not exactly three, use each recording's full options.
If the shared-word pairings fit poorly, compare each recording's full options.
Reusing a word is allowed when the visible evidence supports it. Distinct words remain a useful preference within a credible group, never a forced
schema restriction. Shared similar words alone do not prove membership. Flag weak
routine correspondence and actor deviations without silently regrouping.
Singletons have no cross-recording exclusion evidence; compare all their options.
Familiar option trios in a single question are weak hints, not a safe exclusion rule.
