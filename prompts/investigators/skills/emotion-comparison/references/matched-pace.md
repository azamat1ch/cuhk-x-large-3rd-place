# Compare equivalent work at original timing

## Gather comparable observations

Use overview sheets to find common retrieval/use/release episodes, exercise bouts,
walking sections or transitions. Inspect denser intervals for the closest plausible
swap; use comparable temporal sampling and original timestamps for both recordings.
You can issue separate view calls for members together and reason over their results.
Do not time-warp clips to equal lengths. Sampled images reveal poses and elapsed
intervals, not uninterrupted motion; do not claim fine smoothness beyond the sampling.

For each useful comparison note:
- The action, start/end times, repetitions and whether the work is comparable.
- Movement execution and cadence, separately from stationary holds or pauses.
- Time spent on the action, optional holds or pauses, and delay before the next action.
- Abrupt starts/stops, regularity, placement, corrections and force when visible.

Prefer comparisons supported by two common actions when available. Record both source
length and the observed routine span, from the first relevant action through the last.
Compare how long broadly equivalent work takes, including transitions and meaningful
within-routine pauses. Do not judge overall pace from one isolated stroke or hold.

## Interpret longer duration

Use duration as positive evidence, not something to dismiss automatically. When both
recordings cover comparable work and the finer movement/handling distinction remains
weak, prefer a slow or unhurried option for the longer execution over generic composure,
regularity or task focus. A substantial, repeated difference is stronger evidence than
a tiny length difference. No universal seconds or ratio threshold applies.

Check whether additional required work, unrelated setup, trailing footage or incomplete
streams explain the difference. If they account for it, duration is not a pace tie-breaker.
Separate required workload from open-ended engagement. Washing the face, brushing teeth
and combing hair have no fixed visible repetition quota. Spending longer on these same
stages, including more ordinary strokes, can express taking one's time even when each
stroke has similar speed. Do not automatically subtract those repetitions as a confound
or rename them earnestness. Repeatedly longer bouts across the same routine favor an
unhurried interpretation over one isolated slow implement changeover. Added distinct
tasks or clearly greater required workload remain confounds; finer checking, correction
or unusually purposeful handling can independently support an earnest interpretation.
If repetition counts are uncertain, retain that uncertainty rather than asserting either
slowness or extra work as fact. Never sum overlapping event intervals as elapsed time or
invent a real-world standard time for staged acts. Similar total lengths leave the
local timing and handling comparison decisive.

## Grooming: full cycles and time spent on the main activity

For repetitive grooming, compare a complete cycle (stroke, return, reset and pause),
not only the downward stroke. A small difference of one or two sampled frames, with
overlapping timing ranges or hidden start/end points, is weak evidence for reversing
the broader pace contrast. An apparent extra pass is not a measured explanation of
the entire duration gap. Separate known trailing footage from uncertain within-task
time; do not subtract every hold or reset merely because the hands briefly stop.
If local cycle timing remains ambiguous, prefer Slowly for the more extended comparable
grooming performance and Calmly for the more compact, composed one. Clear repeated
full-cycle timing can override this general preference; for Slowly versus Calmly,
apply the more specific main-activity comparison in the next paragraph. Raw clip
length alone cannot decide either comparison.

When the remaining contrast is specifically Slowly versus Calmly in repetitive
grooming, prioritize the sustained main-action bout over the speed of selected passes.
Time from task-directed gathering/positioning through the final grooming release;
include resets and ordinary in-task holds, exclude standing after completion. A longer
bout with more ordinary passes can enact Slowly at the activity level even if the
shorter, composed performance has slower individual descents. Do not reverse this
assignment solely because the shorter bout contains fewer, longer strokes. To override
the main-bout contrast, require a broader pattern of drawn-out execution, not just
pass timing plus a slightly longer secondary wrist/implement inspection. If that
broader evidence is absent, prefer Slowly for the more sustained grooming bout and
Calmly for its more compact composed counterpart, and acknowledge the local-speed
counterevidence. This is an interpretation preference, not a claim that all motions
in the selected Slowly take are slower.

## Pairs and other close comparisons

For a compatible pair with a visibly supported brisk/rushed contrast, test whether
the longer take provides a real unhurried contrast. Prefer the offered slow/unhurried
interpretation when equivalent work is drawn out and there is no distinctive evidence for the competing
measured word. Do not force this contrast onto a possible fragment of a larger group.
For two measured members of a triplet, apply the same duration tie-breaker without
requiring a universal slow/medium/fast assignment.

A rushed take need not be frantic or clumsy. Brief time spent on each action, short
bouts of repeated movement and immediate transitions can signal urgency even when
some holds are slow and movements coordinated. Conversely, rapid individual motions
amid long deliberate pauses need not make the whole routine hurried. Compare both action and transition
patterns. Unknown-purpose pauses remain uncertain rather than evidence of leisure.

For the two less-rushed takes, compare both members on the SAME two or more useful
episodes when available. Briefly record movement/cycle timing, within-action dwell,
transition delay and extra work separately. Do not time one member's active stroke
against the other's entire bout. Unknown repetition counts are uncertainty, not a
reason to discard every timing observation. A script-required reading or grooming
hold is weak alone, but repeated differences across actions can support overall
unhurried execution. No requirement that every motion share one speed.

Form a provisional contrast in plain observable language before assigning words.
Then ask whether the preferred adjective explains that contrast better than its
alternative. Apply the same evidence standard to both: ordinary looking-at-the-task,
two-handed support, upright/leaning posture or completing a required step should not
outweigh repeated matched timing unless they reveal a distinctive execution pattern.
An isolated flourish, correction or omitted step can be an actor deviation. Additional
work is not slower motion, but localized repeated coverage can support thorough or
meticulous execution when that is the actual distinction being tested.

Deliberate placement and checking can help distinguish attentive/careful execution;
a glance down or a required exercise hold alone cannot. One initial hesitation or
forgotten step is weaker than a style repeated throughout. Actors may interpret scripts
imperfectly; retain observed deviations instead of requiring textbook performances.

## Choose additional views only for a specific unresolved distinction

IR is useful for handling; plain Depth and Thermal can clarify gross cadence and
posture or recover longer coverage. Establish visual landmarks before cross-source
alignment. Crop/enlarge for a decisive hand interaction, with native context retained.
Repeated cosmetic transforms of the same unresolved movement are not new evidence.
